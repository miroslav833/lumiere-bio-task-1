Image 1 shows two panels from a mouse liver study. Panel A has haematoxylin and eosin stained liver sections of five groups, and each section carries the name of its group above it. Panel B gives the mean serum aspartate aminotransferase and the mean serum alanine aminotransferase of the same five groups, where the group names sit on the horizontal axis and the legend tells which bar belongs to which enzyme.

Find how many groups meet both of the conditions below.

The first condition is about the section. The liver section of the group shows no "macrovesicular steatosis". A section counts as steatotic when large round clear vacuoles sit inside the hepatocytes and take up more than half of the hepatocytes in the field. A clear space which is a vessel lumen or a bile duct is not a vacuole of this kind, so a section which holds only those spaces counts as free of steatosis.

The second condition is about the serum. For that same group the ratio of the mean aspartate aminotransferase to the mean alanine aminotransferase is greater than 2. The axis carries a tick every 100 U/L, so judge each bar against those ticks by eye and take the top of the bar rather than the top of the error bar.

Give the call you made on each section and the two values you read for each group, then put your final answer as a single integer on the last line.
